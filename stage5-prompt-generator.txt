1. WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS (WHAT WE LEARNED; THIS SHAPES THE DESIGN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The system. A player walks forward at constant speed through procedurally generated
backrooms-style hallways and rooms and steers by clicking: only the click's horizontal
position matters, it is held until the next accepted click (debounce 150 ms = 3 frames
at 20 Hz), and the engine resolves it to the nearest reachable doorway with a jerk-limited
turn. The network never sees doors or the engine. The training data is the engine's
rendering of bot-driven episodes: a frame sequence plus, per frame, actionX (the
held click position that drives the transition from this frame to the NEXT) and
actionHasClicked. Dataset bulk_v1: 876,261 frames, 1,445 episodes (train about 1,100;
val and test about 190 each), packed at 96x72 and 64x48; direction balance is 50/50;
val and test each hold more than 11,500 near-door two-door contexts.

The core difficulty (the effect-size problem). At 20 Hz with jerk-limited steering, a
click moves the NEXT frame by only about 0.03 pixel at 64x48. The turn builds up over
about 10 frames (median time to align with a door) to roughly 10 pixels. So the
action's effect on the next frame is far below noise and the context frames already
show any turn in progress; a one-step pixel loss therefore teaches a model to ignore the
click. The baseline model beat "repeat the last frame" by about 3x but ignored actions,
and its rollouts melted into abstract blur within about 10 steps (half a second); it
averaged over unpredictable new content (doorways appearing) instead of generating it.
Its rollouts also drifted toward red; color stability must be watched.

What the Stage 4 probes established (see the report for exact numbers):
  - The conditioning pathway works; the problem is the training signal.
  - Supervising the prediction 10 steps ahead makes the model action-responsive, but
    only when it is given the TRUE future clicks. At play time only the current held
    click is known. Under the deployment-realistic "hold-last" conditioning (future
    action = the last action repeated) the model learned "a turn is coming" but not
    which way.
  - A small network CAN read the turn direction from the context frames plus the clicks
    (probe P4: binary sign accuracy about 0.78-0.85 at 64x48 against an engine-rule
    ceiling of about 0.87), and the biggest single gain came from an explicit
    DeltaActionX input (the change in action, a_t minus a_{t-1}). Resolution (64x48 vs
    96x72) was not the bottleneck.
  - An auxiliary direction head on a pixel-loss decoder fixed the encoder but NOT the
    output frames (P5): a plain decoder under pixel loss never maps "the bottleneck knows
    it is turning left" to a sub-pixel-to-few-pixel global view shift.
  - Handing the network the correct direction as a direct input and making its output
    structured as "warp the last frame by a predicted shift, plus a residual" produced
    correct click-direction behavior about 87% of the time (P6, but only ~30 contexts
    from 15 val episodes, and with an ORACLE direction taken from the engine). When the
    residual was left unconstrained it shadowed the warp (the model drew the image
    through the residual and ignored the shift; performance fell to chance level).
  - Never yet tested: the same thing with the direction supplied by the LEARNED
    readout (no oracle); steering sustained over many frames; and any of this inside
    a generative model.
So the design direction is: a learned direction/ego-motion readout (supervised from the
engine's ground truth during training only) feeds a structured motion output, and a
stochastic generative component creates new content (doorways, rooms, far ends) without
being able to override the steering.

Facts for planning: the baseline (a 708k-parameter residual U-Net) runs in well under
1 ms per frame, so there is large headroom for a heavier few-step generator. The target
is real-time play (at least 15 frames per second, aim for 25+) on the laptop RTX 5070 at
96x72, including everything in the generation loop. Loader throughput on bulk 96x72 with
K = 4 is about 3,800 windows/s (2 workers) to 7,700 (4 workers). The bulk 96x72 set is
18 GB and does not fit in RAM comfortably alongside everything else; use the memory-mapped
loader as it is.

2. NON-NEGOTIABLES: WHAT MUST BE TRUE OF THE FINISHED SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the definition of "authentic" for this project. Everything else is your call.
  N1. At play time the model's ONLY inputs are: its own recent generated frames (plus an
      initial seed context of real frames to start from) and the click-derived signals
      (actionX, actionHasClicked, and anything derived from the click history such as
      DeltaActionX or frames-since-click). Nothing else.
  N2. At play time nothing from the engine is available: no door positions, no sidecar
      fields, no ground-truth heading, no room or entity labels. You MAY supervise
      internal heads (direction, ego-motion, door-position) from the sidecar DURING
      TRAINING, and the warp operation is a differentiable part of the network, which is
      fine. Prove N1/N2 with a test (Section 7, item 5).
  N3. Trained from scratch. No pretrained weights, no downloaded models, no fine-tuning of
      someone else's checkpoint.
  N4. No clean categorical labels as network inputs (room type, "door ahead", color-coded
      exits, and so on). The user plans later versions where spaces become undefined
      and dreamlike; the design must not depend on classifying the world into clean
      categories. Learned continuous internal representations are fine.
  N5. The generative part is genuinely stochastic and free to invent content. Different
      random seeds from the same context should give different (plausible) new content
      where something new is revealed. Steering, by contrast, should be controlled
      by the click.
  N6. No retrieval or replay: at play time the system must not paste in dataset frames or
      patches (other than the initial seed context). All pixels after the seed come out
      of the network.
  N7. The generator must run in real time on this laptop (Gate G4). A slower model that
      looks better is a research result, not the deliverable.

3. WORK PLAN: MILESTONES AND GATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Architecture, loss functions, K, flow/shift representation, sampler, number of denoising
steps, training recipe and tricks are YOURS to decide within Section 2; document them
and the reasons. Develop at 64x48 first, then train the final recipe at 96x72. Use mirror
augmentation (flip the image, negate actionX and DeltaActionX; the engine's world is
left/right symmetric and the bulk turn balance is 50/50). Sample training windows so
that click onsets and retargets and door-reveal moments are well represented, while
steady walking, the never-clicked regime (actionHasClicked = 0, where the engine steers
toward the straightest door by itself) and the stale-held-click regime (the player
stopped clicking) all stay in the training mix. Drop viewUnsafe windows as the loader
does. Select models on the validation split only; the test split is touched ONCE, at the
end of Milestone 4.

The evaluation power available now is large (val has about 190 episodes and over 11,500
near-door two-door contexts). Use at least 300 qualifying contexts drawn from at least
100 distinct val episodes, mid-episode (never consecutive starts of one episode), for
every steering gate, and report near-door and far-door contexts separately. Reuse the
qualifying-context definition in neural/probe_click.py.

--- MILESTONE 1: THE STEERING CORE (deterministic; no generative part yet) ---
Goal: show that, under PLAY-TIME conditions (hold-last: only the current click and past
frames and clicks are known; a click arriving at the most recent step), a network with
NO oracle inputs turns the view the right way, by the right amount, and keeps doing so
for many frames.
 Build: (a) a learned readout of the intended turn (direction and magnitude, and enough
 of its time course to drive the next several frames) from context frames plus click
 history, including an explicit DeltaActionX-type input; supervised from the sidecar
 (training only); (b) a structured motion output driven by that readout (a predicted
 horizontal shift or low-resolution flow applied to the last frame by differentiable
 resampling, handling both the turn and the constant forward motion) plus a residual
 that is constrained so it cannot shadow the motion (for example gated, regularized,
 or deferred to the generator in Milestone 2); (c) the evaluation of Gate G1.
 Known hazard to design for: the per-frame shift is a fraction of a pixel, and
 repeatedly resampling an already-resampled image blurs it, accumulating over a rollout.
 Measure sharpness versus rollout length for this component and choose a mitigation
 (for example accumulating the fractional offset, a better resampling scheme, or
 relying on the generator in Milestone 2 to restore detail); document the choice.
 First, for a quick reference, evaluate the existing pilot-trained Stage 4 checkpoints
 on the new, much larger val set and note how the earlier headline numbers hold up at
 scale (cheap; do not retrain them).
 GATE G1 (hard), on the new val set under hold-last with the LEARNED readout (no oracle):
   - click-counterfactual (left door vs right door vs no click, probe_click.py style):
     both-correct at least 0.70 on near-door contexts (chance is 0.25), per-side direction
     accuracy reported, far-door contexts reported separately;
   - predicted-versus-real shift ratio at a 10-step horizon at least 0.5 (it was about
     0.08 for the Stage 4 lookahead model);
   - sustained steering: over 40 steps after a click, with the true click sequence from
     the real clip supplied (hold-last, closed loop on the model's own frames), the
     model's cumulative image shift (phase-correlation estimate, as in the harness's
     action-following metric) versus the real clip's: sign agreement at least 0.8 and a
     median ratio between 0.6 and 1.4 on onset windows; and the view must stop turning
     when the real one does (no runaway drift) — report the overshoot;
   - readout direction accuracy (sign) reported against the engine-rule ceiling.
 If G1 is missed, try in order (each a short, well-chosen experiment): longer or
 richer click-history features; a larger readout trained with the multi-step shift
 supervision; a supervised door-position head (a learned network predicting where doors
 are in the frame, trained from the sidecar's candidates, feeding the readout; this
 is a legitimate learned perception module, not an oracle). If G1 is still missed,
 CHECK-IN with numbers and options, including an engine-side steering responsiveness
 change (a tuning knob that affects how turns feel; the user decides; do not touch
 the engine).

--- MILESTONE 2: THE GENERATOR (stochastic, few-step) ---
Goal: replace "blurry average" with sampled, sharp new content while keeping the
steering of Milestone 1 intact.
 Build a stochastic generative model (diffusion or flow-matching style; your choice)
 for the next frame that is conditioned on the recent frames, the click-derived signals,
 and the structured motion from Milestone 1 (for instance the motion-warped last frame
 plus the readout features), so the generator mostly has to invent what is newly
 revealed and sharpen what the warp blurs, while the motion carries the steering.
 Design for at most about 4 sampling steps per frame from the start (the real-time
 budget); you may consider distillation only if you find it necessary. Expect to need
 tricks for autoregressive stability that are standard in this field: noise
 augmentation of the context frames during training, training on the model's own
 outputs for part of the time, and short context. Conditioning dropout with guidance on
 the action is an option if the model under-uses direction, but steering must come
 primarily from the structured motion path, not from the denoiser alone.
 Train from scratch at 64x48, then the final recipe at 96x72.
 GATE G2 (hard for steering, targets for quality), on val:
   - steering: the Gate G1 numbers must still hold WITH the generator in the loop
     (same thresholds, several sampling seeds; report variance across seeds);
   - one-step quality: report pixel metrics versus the Stage 4 baseline, but also
     sharpness (for example mean gradient magnitude or Laplacian variance) and
     per-channel color statistics against real frames; the generator's sharpness should
     be within about 25% of the real frames' and well above the baseline's; do not
     judge the generator mainly by L1, since a sharp-but-different frame scores worse
     than a blurry average on L1 by design;
   - diversity: at doorway-reveal moments, different seeds should give visibly
     different content; report a simple measure of seed-to-seed variation versus
     steady-walking moments (it should be larger at reveals);
   - rollout to 60 steps with the true clicks: report sharpness, color statistics, and
     the shift metrics against horizon, and the horizon at which the model stops
     looking like real frames by these statistics.
 CHECK-IN 1 (mandatory) at the end of this milestone; see Section 5.

--- MILESTONE 3: LONG-HORIZON STABILITY ---
Goal: the model must keep producing plausible, steerable hallways for a long time in
closed loop, since real play is open-ended.
 Run closed-loop rollouts of 300 frames (15 seconds) from real seed contexts, on at least
 30 val clips, in three scenarios: (1) no clicks at all (the engine's default behavior is
 to drift toward the straightest door; the model learned this from data; check it keeps
 walking sensibly), (2) the real click sequence of the source episode, and (3) a scripted
 alternating left/right click pattern with reasonable spacing. Track against horizon:
 sharpness, per-channel color statistics (including any drift toward red), brightness
 and contrast, the fraction of nearly-black or saturated frames, the apparent motion
 (shift) statistics, and frame-to-frame change (to catch flicker or freezing). Fix what
 degrades: exposure-bias mitigations (more training on own outputs, noise augmentation
 levels, context length), color stabilization, whatever you find. Do not "fix" the
 world's content by adding any engine information.
 Optional if cheap and informative: train a small real-versus-generated frame
 classifier and report the horizon at which generated frames become distinguishable.
 GATE G3 (targets; report honestly if missed): through 300 frames in all three
 scenarios, sharpness within about 25% of real, per-channel mean within about 0.03 (on
 a 0-1 scale) of the real distribution, no collapse to black/white/uniform frames, no
 frozen image (motion statistics comparable to real), and the Gate G1 steering behavior
 still holding in scenario 2 and 3 at 100+ frames in. If these cannot be met, document
 the failure mode precisely (this is itself a result for the user's write-up) and
 CHECK-IN with options only if you believe a different approach (not more tuning) is
 required.

--- MILESTONE 4: FINAL EVALUATION, SPEED, AND THE HARDWARE ANALYSIS ---
 Do once, with the final model at 96x72:
  - Evaluate the final model on the TEST split (once): the steering gates, the quality
    and diversity metrics, and 300-frame closed-loop stability.
  - Real-time Gate G4 (hard): the complete per-frame generation loop (readout, motion,
    all sampling steps, decode to a displayable uint8 image, with CUDA synchronized)
    at batch size 1 on this GPU on mains power: at least 15 frames/s sustained over
    1,000 frames (report mean, p95 and worst frame latency), aim for 25+. Peak
    inference VRAM under about 3 GB. If a model slightly above budget is much better,
    report both; ship the one that meets G4. If G4 cannot be met, CHECK-IN with the
    measured breakdown and options (fewer steps, smaller denoiser, lower precision,
    CUDA graphs or compilation, and so on).
  - Measurements for the user's neuromorphic/unconventional-computing class: parameter
    count; multiply-adds per generated frame and the share contributed by each component
    (readout, motion, denoiser per step, decoder); numeric precision used; memory
    traffic or activation memory per frame if you can estimate it cheaply; the fraction
    of operations that are dense convolution/matmul versus anything else; the measured
    sparsity of activations in the trained network (share of exact or near zeros after its
    nonlinearities); the measured frame-to-frame sparsity of the data and of generated
    frames (share of pixels changing by more than a small threshold between consecutive
    frames); and the latency breakdown. State what these suggest about hardware
    (dense tensor accelerator versus event-driven or in-memory designs) in two or three
    technical paragraphs. Keep it measured, not speculative.

--- MILESTONE 5: MINIMAL INTERACTIVE PLAYER ---
 Required if Gate G4 is met (otherwise skip and say so). A thin, separate client script
 (the engine is NOT involved) that: loads the final checkpoint; picks a seed context of K
 real frames from the dataset's early-episode frames (random per launch) and generates
 from there; shows the generated frames in a window at an enlarged size; reads the mouse,
 where a click sets the held signal exactly the way the engine does (X = clamp((clientX -
 left) / width * 2 - 1, -1, 1), left is negative; a click closer than 3 frames (150 ms)
 after the last ACCEPTED click is ignored for the held signal; actionHasClicked becomes 1
 after the first accepted click); draws its own brief translucent click pulse over the
 displayed image (the pulse is NOT part of the generated frames and must never be fed back
 to the model); shows an fps readout; and offers a scripted headless mode that plays a
 given click sequence for N frames and writes a video or frame strip (used for your tests
 and for the user's review). Keep dependencies light and pip-installable inside the venv;
 no sudo. You cannot judge the live feel; verify the headless mode and that the
 interactive window opens and runs, then hand it to the user.
