1. BIG PICTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. This engine (TypeScript / three.js / Puppeteer) generates episodes of
     (frames, control signal). It is a teacher and is discarded afterwards.
  2. A separate Python/PyTorch codebase (future, in /neural) will train an
     action-conditioned next-frame model: given recent frames plus the control
     signal, predict the next frame. At play time a thin client feeds the
     player's clicks to the model; this engine is NOT running.
  3. Later (not in this stage) the user will record fresh human sessions as a
     held-out test set. The user's CURRENT playtest recordings are throwaway
     debug data that they delete and recreate; ignore them entirely and do not
     modify the recorder.

Consequences that drive this stage:
  - The model learns p(next frame | past frames, signal). The bots do not need to
    click like a human. They must (a) COVER every situation a human could create
    and (b) make the signal carry information the frames do not already carry.
  - Point (b) is critical. If bots mostly click the straightest door, the click
    becomes a near-deterministic function of what is on screen; the model can
    predict the camera's motion from pixels alone, learn to ignore the signal, and
    then not respond when a real player clicks a different door (causal
    confusion). At moments of real choice, the bot's choice must be spread
    roughly uniformly over the reachable doors, regardless of how central a door
    is. The old placeholder chose the straightest door in roughly 90% of choice
    frames (Section 2.6).
  - The engine maps a click to the nearest door by screen X, so signal ->
    outcome is piecewise constant: plateaus (all X nearest door A) separated by
    boundaries midway between doors, plus fallback when a door is unreachable.
    The informative clicks are near boundaries, at the screen extremes, on walls,
    and late in an approach, not only on door centers.
  - The training frames must be pure, continuous, deterministic renderings of
    the simulation: nothing time-dependent, no UI, no pops that the simulation
    state does not explain (4.5), and no clipping or wall-crash frames (4.11).

2. VERIFIED FACTS ABOUT THE ENGINE (from the read-only investigation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Line numbers are from commit 105f8e4 and may have shifted.

2.1 Input and signal semantics
  - The only input is the pointer `click` event, and only its X is used.
    screenX = clamp((clientX - rect.left) / rect.width * 2 - 1, -1, 1); left is
    negative; linear in screen pixels; based on the canvas (320 px wide), not
    affected by RENDER_SCALE. Doorway screen X comes from the same camera/FOV via
    full perspective projection of the doorway's APPROACH POINT.
  - Engine.submitClick stores a pending click; it is processed at the start of
    the next Engine.step() via Steering.handleClick.
  - Debounce is 0.150 s = 3 frames at 20 Hz. A click inside the window changes
    neither the target nor the held signal (the click pulse still fires). Only
    accepted clicks update the held signal.
  - controlSignalX = X of the most recent ACCEPTED click (a held step function),
    0 before the first accepted click. In the headless manifest "never clicked"
    and "clicked dead center" are both 0 and cannot be told apart.
  - STICKY target: after an accepted click the target doorway is resolved once and
    held until (a) a new accepted click arrives, or (b) the target is no longer a
    live doorway of the active entity (the player walked through it); it is then
    re-resolved from the HELD X against the new entity's candidates. So the held
    X persists across entity boundaries and keeps steering; it does not reset.
    With NO click yet, the target is re-resolved EVERY frame to the reachable
    candidate nearest screen center.
  - Resolution: stable-sort candidates by |screenX - referenceX|; the nearest
    reachable wins; otherwise search outward in distance order, same side first,
    then the other side; otherwise keep the previous target if still live, else
    null (the player keeps its heading).
  - "Reachable" = the approach point is inside the 75-degree front cone AND the
    distance is at least the jerk-limited arc length needed to finish the turn.
    The same click can be accepted early and fall back to another door when late.
  - Retargeting is allowed at any time, including mid-turn; only debounce gates
    it. Walking speed is constant (4.0 units/s). About 10.7% of frames have zero
    reachable candidates (the player then holds its heading).

2.2 Timing and alignment (the off-by-one that must be fixed in the labels)
  - Headless loop per iteration i: the bot decides a click; capture frame i; read
    getState; write the PNG; record controlSignalX FROM THE STATE BEFORE THE STEP;
    then step(clickX), which applies the click and advances one timestep.
  - So a click made at iteration i first shows up in controlSignalX of frame i+1,
    and the heading first changes at frame i+1. The transition from frame i to
    frame i+1 is driven by controlSignalX[i+1], NOT controlSignalX[i]. A
    next-frame model conditioned on controlSignalX[i] to predict frame i+1 would
    use a stale signal. (Confirmed by a scripted test.)
  - Latency after an accepted click: the heading starts changing within 1-2
    frames (median 2); alignment to the target takes a median of about 10 frames;
    about 14% of clicks produce no visible turn within 60 frames (door already
    almost straight ahead).
  - simulatedTime == frameIndex * 0.05 exactly; no duplicated or skipped frames.
    The final step's result is discarded: a 600-frame episode has 600 frames and
    599 transitions. Headless simulation is fixed-timestep and does not depend on
    wall-clock time; flicker is off.

2.3 World generation and determinism
  - Entities are kind 'hallway' | 'room'. A hallway ends in a bend, a junction,
    or a room (terminations, not separate kinds). The first entity id is h1.
  - World content is a function of (seed, PATH), not seed alone: one shared
    seeded PRNG plus lazy generation means contents depend on the order in which
    entities are generated. Two policies on the same seed share the start hallway
    and diverge once their paths diverge. Within one path, runs are exactly
    reproducible (two headless runs with one seed gave byte-identical PNGs and
    bit-identical state on this machine's NVIDIA/Vulkan path).
  - The start hallway is random per seed. No Math.random in world/steering/bot
    code (seeded mulberry32). The only nondeterminism is episodeId (randomUUID).
  - World.maintain(activeId) generates not-yet-generated neighbors of the active
    entity and disposes everything else. Second-degree doorways are deterministic
    solid-black quads (blackout), not fog.

2.4 Pipeline structure
  - The placeholder bot runs in Node as a pure function (frameIndex) => number |
    null, clicking uniform random X every 20-80 frames. The pipeline crosses the
    Puppeteer bridge three times per frame (captureFrame, getState, step) plus a
    Node-side PNG write. getState ALREADY returns, every frame: player
    {x,z,heading,angularVelocity}, activeEntityId, frameIndex, simulatedTime,
    controlSignalX, wallOverlap, collisionEvents, lastClickedX, targetDoorwayId,
    targetPoint, targetPhase, and candidates[] ({doorwayId, screenX, reachable,
    screenDistance}). A bot given that already-read state costs no extra bridge
    calls.
  - CLI today: --seed is a BASE (episode seeds are base+i); episode ids are random
    UUIDs; no explicit seed list; no per-episode policy name; resolution and all
    other config are compile-time constants.
  - The manifest is written once at the end. A crash at frame 500/600 leaves 500
    PNGs and no manifest; nothing resumes; no completion marker.
  - Headless uses the NVIDIA dGPU (Vulkan, GPU_LAUNCH_ARGS).
  - The data directory is engine/data/ (gitignored), not /data as the README says.
  - Headless manifest today (implicit schema version 1): episode level
    {episodeId, renderWidth/Height, outputWidth/Height, simTimestep, seed, config
    subset, frames[]}; frame level {frameIndex, simulatedTime, imagePath,
    controlSignalX, playerX, playerZ, playerHeading, currentEntityId}.

2.5 Performance and size (measured on this machine)
  - Concurrency 1/2/4/6/8 gave aggregate 113 / 213 / 374 / 378 / 523 frames/s;
    about 82 MiB of GPU memory per instance (654 MiB at 8, of 8 GiB). 24 CPU
    cores, 30 GiB RAM. 500,000 frames take roughly 16-22 minutes. Generation is
    CHEAP; do not optimize it.
  - PNG frames average about 80.5 KB; a 600-frame episode is about 48 MB. About
    569 GB of disk are free.

2.6 Statistics observed with the old bots (200 seeds x 1200 frames, replica)
  - Frame share: hallways 64% (bend-ended 21.5%, junction-ended 24.7%, room-ended
    17.8%), rooms 36%. Visit length in frames (median / p10 / p90): hallway-bend
    63/32/131, hallway-junction 75/33/140, hallway-room 60/31/125, room 75/51/99.
  - Reachable candidates per frame: 0 in about 10.7%, exactly 1 in about 44%, 2 or
    more in about 45%.
  - With 2+ reachable candidates, the chosen door was the straightest in 89.9% of
    frames (placeholder bot) and 92.8% (a bot picking a uniformly random reachable
    door once per entity entry; that bot chose only at entry, when usually just
    one door is reachable yet).
  - Fallback (click resolved to a different door than the nearest by screen X):
    2.8% of accepted clicks. Wall contact (slide backstop): about 45-51 per 1,000
    frames; zero wall-overlap frames; zero pinned episodes.

4.1 Action-aligned labels (the off-by-one fix)
  Add explicit per-frame fields so no consumer has to remember the offset:
    - actionX: the held control signal IN EFFECT during the simulation step from
      this frame to the next, i.e. the value controlSignalX shows on the next
      frame. For the final frame of an episode it is null.
    - actionHasClicked: whether at least one click had been ACCEPTED as of that
      same step (false = the engine is in its "never clicked" default mode).
  Keep controlSignalX unchanged for compatibility, but document it as "the signal
  at capture time" and state plainly that training should use actionX.

4.2 Click-event logging
  The headless manifest cannot currently show debounced clicks. Add, aligned to
  the same step as actionX (the click submitted for the step from this frame to
  the next):
    - clickX: the RAW click X submitted for that step, or null.
    - clickAccepted: true if the engine accepted it, false if debounce ignored it,
      null if there was no click.
  This requires the step path to report whether a click was accepted.

4.5 Training-frame cleanliness audit (overlay, lag, continuity)

  Audit three things in the HEADLESS capture path. Use code reading plus small
  numeric checks; do NOT do your own visual review (Rules of Engagement 3).
  (a) UI overlay. The click pulse is UI feedback, not part of the world. Determine

      Training frames must be overlay-free (the future real-time client will draw
      its own overlay). If any overlay is in the captured pixels, move it so human

      immediately.
  (b) Lag must not reach the data. The user notices a brief hitch in live play when
      entering a new room (presumably entity generation/disposal done

      whether any such effect can reach the dataset. Confirm that nothing in the

4.6 Click-bot framework
  Replace the single placeholder bot with a small bot framework.

  Interface. A bot receives the frame index and the same state snapshot the logger
  reads that frame, and returns a click X in [-1,1] or null. The pipeline reads
  getState first, then asks the bot, then steps, so bots add no bridge calls. Bots
  run in Node and keep their own memory (for example the previous frame's active
  entity id, to know when an entity was entered). Bot randomness comes from its own
  seeded generator derived from the episode seed, entirely separate from the
  world's generator. If a persona needs extra read-only observations (for example
  distance to a candidate's approach point), you may add read-only fields to
  getState; do not change engine behavior.

  Two regimes must both appear in the data:
    (i) "never clicked": default mode, which exists only at the start of an episode
        until the first accepted click. So first-click time varies widely: about 10%
        of episodes never click at all; the others draw a first-click frame from a
        broad distribution (frame 0 up to a few hundred). All personas are
        suppressed until then.
    (ii) "stale held X": the player has clicked in the past and then stops clicking
        while the held X and sticky target keep steering through new entities.

  A "choice moment" is a frame where 2 or more candidates are reachable, or the
  moment the reachable set gains a member. Decisive behavior is triggered by choice
  moments and by entity entry, NOT only once at entity entry, because many choices
  appear mid-approach.

  Personas (parameters, delays, and weights live in config; times are frames at
  20 Hz; debounce is 3 frames):
    1. idle: no clicks for a segment (the stale-held-X regime).
    2. decisive: at a choice moment, wait a human-like reaction delay (initial guess
       uniform 4-25 frames), then pick UNIFORMLY among the currently reachable
       candidates (not the straightest) and click at that candidate's screenX plus
       small Gaussian jitter (initial sigma 0.03-0.10, clamped to [-1,1]). At most
       one click per choice moment.
    3. late: like decisive but with long delays so the click comes when the door is
       close and reachability is marginal (exercises the fallback behavior).
    4. dither: choose a door, then after roughly 10-40 frames re-click toward a
       DIFFERENT reachable door (sometimes flipping back), respecting debounce.
    5. prober: deliberately informative clicks at choice moments: the midpoint
       between two adjacent candidates (plateau boundaries), the extremes (-1, +1),
       exactly 0, and positions far from any candidate (wall clicks).
    6. spammer: bursts of 2-8 clicks 1-2 frames apart (some inside the debounce
       window, some not), at random or alternating X, then 20-80 frames of silence.
    7. uniform: click X ~ U(-1,1) at random intervals of 20-80 frames (the old
       placeholder behavior, kept as an exploration floor).
  Segmenting: an episode is divided into segments of random length (initial guess
  100-300 frames); each segment draws a persona from the active mixture profile.
  Mixture profiles, selectable by name in a run plan:
    - mixture (default): idle 10, decisive 30, late 10, dither 10, prober 15,
      spammer 5, uniform 20.
    - uniformOnly: persona 7 only.
    - doorAwareOnly: personas 2, 3, 4 with weights 3:1:1.
  (The named profiles exist so the user can later generate ablation datasets
  cheaply.)

  Success criterion (reported, tuned at most once): among frames with 2+ reachable
  candidates, the share where the CURRENT TARGET is the straightest candidate
  should be far below the old 90-93%. Report it per persona and for the mixture. If
  the mixture is above about 65%, adjust persona parameters or weights and
  regenerate the pilot ONCE, then report whatever results.

4.7 Run plans, splits, and the CLI
  - A plan names the dataset, the mixture profile, frames per episode (default
    600), the output root, and an explicit list of episodes, each with a seed, a
    split name (train, val, or test), and a profile.
  - Seeds for the three splits come from DISJOINT ranges, recorded in the plan.
    Episodes land in <output>/<plan name>/<split>/<deterministic episode dir>/.
  - The CLI can generate a plan from a few parameters (counts per split, seed
    ranges, frames, profile) and run a plan with resume. Keep the old flags
    working. Default concurrency for plan runs: 8 (overridable).
  - Pilot plan: 120 train, 15 val, 15 test episodes, 600 frames each.

4.8 Diagnostics report
  A script that reads a dataset's manifests (not the PNGs) and writes a JSON file
  plus a Markdown summary with exactly these items:
    1. Totals: episodes, frames, bytes on disk, mean PNG size.
    2. Histogram (0.1 bins) of ACCEPTED click values at the click-event level, a
       separate histogram of the HELD signal per frame, and the share of frames with
       actionHasClicked=false.
    3. Reachable-candidate count histogram and the share of frames with 2+.
    4. Straightest-target rate among frames with 2+ reachable candidates, per
       persona and overall.
    5. Intent-honored rate per persona, and the engine fallback rate.
    6. Label ambiguity: the share of frames where the engine's actual target differs
       from what a STATELESS resolution of the held X over that frame's candidates
       would pick (how much hidden state the action label does not capture).
    7. Collision events per 1,000 frames and wall-overlap frames.
    8. Per-persona frame share and click rate.
    9. Integrity: incomplete episodes, and any seed shared between splits.
   10. Clipping failsafe (4.11): aborted-episode count and share overall and per
       persona, frames trimmed, and the share of frames with a brief graze (L3).

4.11 Clipping failsafe (headless agent episodes only)
  Goal: no clipping or wall-crash frames may reach the dataset. The simulator is
  not perfect (the user occasionally crashes into a wall while playing) and there is
  no time to fix every edge case, so this is a SAFETY NET, not a fix. Do NOT try to
  fix the underlying causes of wall contact in this stage; note any causes you
  observe in the stage report. Human interactive play is unaffected.

  What counts as clipping. Detection uses engine state (never image analysis) and
  is evaluated every frame. Define three levels, and propose exact thresholds at
  Check-in 1 after measuring (Section 5, step A3b):
    - L1 hard clip: the player or camera is inside wall geometry or off the
      walkable floor (wallOverlap), or so close to a wall that the view could show
      through it (closer than the camera's near-clip distance plus a small margin).
    - L2 sustained contact: the wall-slide backstop is engaged for N consecutive
      frames (starting guess N = 10, i.e. 0.5 s), meaning the player is grinding or
      pinned along a wall.
    - L3 brief graze: the slide backstop is engaged for fewer than N frames while
      the camera stays clear of walls. NOT a crash: counted and reported only.
  The failsafe fires on L1 or L2. Important measured fact: with the old bots the
  slide backstop engaged in about 45-51 of every 1,000 frames (about 5% of frames;
  the worst episode had roughly 1,000 events in 1,200 frames) while wall-overlap
  occurred in 0 frames. Triggering on ANY slide contact would therefore abort most
  episodes, which is why L3 is not a trigger. Measure how many episodes each level
  would abort before settling on thresholds.

  When it fires:
    1. Stop that agent's episode immediately.
    2. Let c be the first frame of the offending event (for L2, the first frame of
       the contact run, not the frame at which N was reached). Remove every frame
       from c - T onward, where T = CLIP_TRIM_SECONDS (config; default 8 s =
       160 frames; 5 s is an acceptable alternative). Physically DELETE the trimmed
       PNGs from disk (do not merely leave them out of the manifest), recompute the
       action-aligned fields so the new final frame has actionX = null, and write the
       manifest as complete with truncated: true, truncationReason, crashFrameIndex,
       and trimmedFrames.
    3. If fewer than MIN_KEPT_FRAMES (config; default 60) remain, delete the whole
       episode directory.
    4. Append a record to <plan dir>/aborted.jsonl containing: episode id, seed,
       split, the persona active at the crash, the level and reason, the crash frame,
       the number of frames kept, the click sequence up to the crash (so the crash
       can be reproduced later for debugging), and a compact state dump (position,
       heading, entity id, target) for the last ~20 frames. An aborted episode counts
       as done for resume purposes; it is never retried automatically and is not
       replaced.
  Why there is no live delay buffer: nothing streams to a model. Episodes are written
  to disk and consumed later, so trimming on disk after detection is equivalent to an
  8-second delay buffer, with no detection latency and less machinery.

  Tell the user if it is frequent. It should be rare. At the end of every plan run,
  print a clear summary line with the aborted count and share and the L3 graze rate.
  If the aborted share exceeds CLIP_ALERT_SHARE (config; default 5%, once at least 20
  episodes have finished), print a prominent warning, exit with a non-zero status,
  and flag it in the report. If it exceeds 25% after at least 20 episodes, stop the
  batch early. Report abort rates per persona so a persona that steers into walls
  stands out.

8. CONSTRAINTS AND DECISION PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Never touch git (Rules of Engagement 1). Never commit generated data.
  - DO NOT change simulation behavior: world generation, steering, reachability,
    debounce, collision, timing, or RNG consumption. The ONLY rendering change you
    may make without approval is removing UI overlays from captured frames (4.5a).
    Adding logging, read-only getState fields, and bot code must not alter what the
    engine computes or the order or number of draws from the world's PRNG; the pilot
    must be exactly reproducible from its plan.
  - If you find an engine bug, a doc/code mismatch that suggests a lost fix (for
    example the missing side-doorway/junction gate radii in 2.7), or anything that
    would require a behavior change, do NOT fix it silently. Record it in the stage
    report under "Needs user decision" with evidence and a suggested fix, correct
    the documentation to match what the code actually does, and continue (or raise
    Check-in 3 if it blocks you).
  - The clipping failsafe and its logging read engine state only (read-only getState
    additions are fine) and must not alter simulation behavior. The failsafe is the
    intended mitigation for wall contact in this stage; do not go fixing the
    underlying edge cases.
  - Out of scope: any neural-network or Python code; dataset packaging into arrays
    or shards (next stage); bulk generation beyond the pilot; changing resolution;
    internet textures or props; changing the signal's definition or the click
    semantics; optimizing generation speed; anything involving the playtest
    recorder, the user's recordings, replaying human sessions, or human click
    statistics.
